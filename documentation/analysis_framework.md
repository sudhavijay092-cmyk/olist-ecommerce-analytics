# SwiftCart — Analysis Framework

## 1. Objective

The analysis will identify the major drivers of contribution-margin
performance and determine which commercial and operational areas require
management attention.

The analysis will not assume that any particular driver is responsible
for poor profitability before the data is analysed.

---

# 2. Primary Analytical Question

Where is SwiftCart losing contribution margin, and which operational
and commercial actions should management investigate first?

---

# 3. Profitability Investigation

The first step is to calculate:

- GMV
- Net Revenue
- Contribution Profit
- Contribution Margin %

The analysis will then compare profitability across time, customers,
products, campaigns, delivery operations and dark stores.

---

# 4. Revenue Investigation

If contribution profit changes, investigate whether revenue performance
is responsible.

Questions:

1. Is order volume increasing or decreasing?
2. Is average order value changing?
3. Is net revenue growing at the same rate as GMV?
4. Are cancellations reducing realized revenue?

---

# 5. Discount Investigation

Investigate whether discounts are reducing contribution profit.

Questions:

1. Which campaigns provide the largest discounts?
2. Which campaigns have the lowest contribution margin?
3. Which customer segments receive the highest discounts?
4. Are high-discount orders generating sufficient contribution profit?
5. Are some products being disproportionately discounted?

---

# 6. Delivery Investigation

Investigate delivery economics.

Questions:

1. Which zones have the highest delivery cost?
2. Does delivery distance increase delivery cost?
3. Which dark stores have the highest delivery delays?
4. Does peak-hour demand affect delivery performance?
5. Do expensive deliveries generate enough order value to justify their
   cost?

---

# 7. Refund and Cancellation Investigation

Investigate financial leakage caused by failed or problematic orders.

Questions:

1. Which cancellation reasons occur most frequently?
2. Which products have high refund rates?
3. Does late delivery increase refund probability?
4. Which dark stores have unusually high refund rates?
5. How much contribution profit is lost through refunds and compensation?

---

# 8. Customer Profitability Investigation

Investigate whether high-revenue customers are also profitable.

Customer analysis should consider:

- Order frequency
- Net revenue
- Discounts
- Refunds
- Delivery costs
- Marketing costs
- Contribution profit
- Contribution margin

Important customer segments may include:

- High-value profitable
- High-value low-margin
- Discount-dependent
- Low-value
- Infrequent customers

The actual segment definitions will be finalized after inspecting the
data.

---

# 9. Product Profitability Investigation

Investigate products and categories based on economics rather than sales
volume alone.

Questions:

1. Which categories generate the most revenue?
2. Which categories generate the most contribution profit?
3. Which categories have weak contribution margins?
4. Which categories have high refund rates?
5. Which products combine high sales volume with poor profitability?

---

# 10. Dark-Store Investigation

Compare dark stores using:

- Orders
- Revenue
- Contribution profit
- Contribution margin
- On-time delivery rate
- Average delivery time
- Refund rate
- Support ticket rate
- Delivery cost

The goal is to identify operationally weak locations.

---

# 11. Marketing Investigation

Evaluate campaigns using economic outcomes rather than revenue alone.

Questions:

1. Which campaigns generate the highest contribution profit?
2. Which campaigns generate high revenue but weak contribution margin?
3. Which customer segments receive the most promotional spending?
4. Does campaign spending appear concentrated on already-active customers?
5. Which campaigns should be investigated for redesign or reduction?

Marketing attribution will be treated as an assumption and clearly
documented.

---

# 12. Root-Cause Framework

When a profitability problem is identified, investigate it in this order:

### Step 1 — Confirm the KPI

Verify the change in:

- Contribution Profit
- Contribution Margin %

### Step 2 — Identify the largest driver

Check:

- Revenue
- Discounts
- Product Cost
- Delivery Cost
- Refunds
- Marketing
- Compensation

### Step 3 — Segment the driver

Break it down by:

- Time
- Customer
- Product
- Category
- Dark Store
- Delivery Zone
- Campaign

### Step 4 — Investigate operational relationships

Where relevant, examine relationships such as:

- Delivery delay → Refunds
- Delivery delay → Reviews
- Distance → Delivery Cost
- Store load → Delivery Delay
- Discount → Contribution Margin
- Product Category → Refund Rate

### Step 5 — Determine business significance

Prioritize problems based on:

- Financial impact
- Frequency
- Operational controllability
- Customer impact

### Step 6 — Recommend action

Recommendations will only be made after the relevant evidence has been
calculated from the final validated dataset.

---

# 13. Decision Priority Matrix

Potential issues will eventually be classified using:

| Impact | Controllability | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | Immediate |
| High | Low | Strategic |
| Low | High | Operational |
| Low | Low | Monitor |

The actual classification will depend on the final analytical findings.

---

# 14. Evidence Standard

Every major recommendation should have:

1. A measurable problem
2. Supporting KPI evidence
3. A segment or operational driver
4. A financial or customer impact
5. A specific management action
6. A metric for measuring whether the action worked

No recommendation should be based solely on a visual correlation or an
assumption.

---

# 15. Final Analytical Output

The final analysis should answer:

1. What is happening?
2. Why is it happening?
3. Where is it happening?
4. Who or what is responsible?
5. How financially significant is it?
6. What should management do?
7. How should management measure success?
